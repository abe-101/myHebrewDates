A Hebrew wedding anniversary is kept on the Hebrew date of the wedding, so it moves to a different English date each year. Calendar apps only repeat events by the English date, so a yearly event set up after the wedding drifts off the right day.

Find the Hebrew date of the wedding

If you know only the English date, use the Hebrew date converter. Many weddings take place in the evening. If the chuppah was after sunset, tick After sunset, because the Hebrew day had already changed.

Add it to your calendar

  1. Sign up and make a calendar.
  2. Add the couple, for example “David & Sarah”, choose Anniversary, and enter the Hebrew date.
  3. On the calendar's Add to your calendar page, pick a reminder time, then choose Google Calendar, Apple Calendar, or Outlook to subscribe.

From then on, the anniversary appears on the right day every year. You can keep birthdays and yahrzeits in the same calendar.

Sharing with family

Send family members the link to the calendar's Add to your calendar page. They don't need an account, and each person picks their own reminder time.

Weddings in Adar

In a Hebrew leap year there are two months of Adar. If the wedding was in a year with a single Adar, choose whether to keep the anniversary in Adar I or Adar II when you add the date.
